## 2) Navigation

**Header**

* Left: Primordial wordmark (deep purple or white with gradient M) in a small, fixed position.
* Right: minimal text nav in Fira Mono, small size:

  * `Thesis`, `System`, `Rights Holders`, `Investors`, `Contact`
* Behavior:

  * Clicking each label scrolls smoothly to the respective section (anchor IDs).
  * The header is either:

    * sticky but slim (thin bar that stays at top on scroll), or
    * static but repeated in a minimal form in the footer (for a more editorial feel).

**In-page navigation**

* Each section has an **anchor** and a matching label in nav.
* Active section indicated by a tiny change:

  * e.g. small dot before the nav link or slight color shift to Lavender Mist.
* Some inline CTAs (e.g. “See how we value a catalog →”) also scroll to the System section.

**Footer structure**

* Single narrow column, same 720px width:

  * Top line: small nav recap: `Thesis / System / Rights / Investors / Contact`
  * Middle: contact information (email, maybe city), Fira Mono.
  * Bottom: tiny copyright + year on the left, and a tiny gradient M icon on the right.
* No social icon strip, no extra sections—keep it austere and editorial.

## Self-check

**3 ways this layout breaks away from generic SaaS landing pages**

1. **Editorial, single-column flow:**
   Instead of multi-column feature grids or cards, everything runs through a **single, narrow, left-aligned column** with margin notes and small labels — more like a long-form thesis or zine than a marketing page.
2. **Symbolic imagery instead of product UI:**
   The hero and backgrounds use the **Giant gradient M and Primordial Soup** as abstract symbols, with no device frames, UI screenshots, or 3D blobs.
3. **Narrative sections instead of blocks:**
   Sections are named and structured like chapters (`The Primordial System`, `For Rights Holders`, `For Investors`, `Next Steps`), with continuous prose and annotated bullets instead of “feature card + testimonial + pricing + FAQ” modules.

**2 spots that might still feel generic & how to make them more characterful**

1. **For Rights Holders / For Investors pair**

   * Risk: Two audience sections back-to-back could still read as a standard “solutions for persona A / persona B.”
   * Refinement: Give each section a **distinct micro-layout flourish**: e.g. a vertical M-shaped rule on the Rights Holders section, and a subtle tick-mark baseline (like a chart axis) behind the Investors section. This keeps structure clear but adds brand-specific visual rhythm.
2. **Contact CTA at the end**

   * Risk: Ending with a “Contact us” block is familiar and could feel like any B2B site.
   * Refinement: Frame the final section more like an **invitation to a working session**—visually emphasize the numbered *“What happens next”* as if it’s an excerpt from a process document, and use a small Fira Mono caption like `Session 01: Valuation Workshop` next to the CTA. That makes the closing feel like a concrete engagement, not just a generic form.
